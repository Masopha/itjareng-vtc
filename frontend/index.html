Our Purpose

Who We Are

Itjareng Vocational Training Centre is a beacon of hope in Lesotho. Founded in 1986 by the Lesotho National Association of the Physically Disabled (LNAPD), we provide inclusive residential vocational training for individuals with physical, intellectual, and hearing disabilities.

We believe in the power of practical skills to transform lives. Our students leave not just with a trade — but with confidence, independence, and a future they built with their own hands.

◆ Open to applicants aged 18–45 years
◆ Residential training — live and learn on-site
◆ M250 registration fee · school fees as per school requirements
◆ Student-made products available for sale
Mission

"To provide practical vocational skills and life skills to disabled individuals so that they can live independently, earn an income, and meaningfully participate in society."

Vision

To be a leading institution in Lesotho that equips people with disabilities with marketable vocational skills, strengthening their ability to compete in the labour market and contribute to economic growth.

Our Story

About IVTC

Nearly four decades of empowering Lesotho's most vulnerable through skills, dignity, and opportunity.

Our History

The Itjareng Vocational Training Centre was established in 1986 by the Lesotho National Association of the Physically Disabled (LNAPD). Born from a recognition that people with disabilities deserved access to practical education and economic participation, IVTC was created by the disabled community, for the disabled community.

Located at Masianokeng Village, approximately 15 km east of Maseru and close to the International Airport, the Centre has over the years served more than 400 learners across a wide range of disabilities. IVTC was legally registered in 1999 and operates as a non-profit organisation.

IVTC is one of very few institutions in Lesotho that accepts students aged 18 to 45 years with no minimum educational requirement — making it truly inclusive and accessible to Lesotho's most marginalized citizens. The centre accommodates between 50 and 70 students at a time, each enrolled in a 2-year residential training program.

Beyond vocational skills, IVTC engages in advocacy, HIV/AIDS awareness training, democracy and human rights education, and reproductive health training — equipping graduates not just with trade skills, but with knowledge of their rights as full members of society.
